DIY OpenTherm adapter for ESP32

Ihor Melnyk's circuit for connecting an ESP32 to the boiler's room-thermostat (OpenTherm) input, Z1 on the ACV ILEA. Two PC817 optocouplers galvanically isolate the boiler from the ESP32. Besides the ESP32 you only need the adapter parts. The LEDs show traffic on the line: red when sending, green when receiving.

Board, component side

A dot-type perfboard seen from the top, the way you insert the parts. Holes are named by row letter and column number (e.g. G5). The ESP32 and four wires are on the right, the cable to the boiler terminals on the left.

Solder side

The same layout with the board flipped, so mirrored: left and right swap. Column numbers flip with the board, so find holes by their label, not by position. The silver lines are wire traces: the easiest is to use clipped resistor leads soldered to consecutive pads.

Identifying loose parts

Resistors, 4 bands

  • 100 Ω: brown, black, brown
  • 220 Ω: red, red, brown
  • 330 Ω: orange, orange, brown
  • 1.5 kΩ: brown, green, red

Resistors, 5 bands (1%)

  • 100 Ω: brown, black, black, black
  • 220 Ω: red, red, black, black
  • 330 Ω: orange, orange, black, black
  • 1.5 kΩ: brown, green, black, brown

Diodes

The glass body of a Zener is marked, e.g. C4V7, 4V3, C15. The values must match. The 1N4148 is a small glass diode; a 1N914 works too. A multimeter in diode mode settles any doubt.

Transistor

Any small PNP from the BC556–BC559 family with the same C-B-E pinout works. Watch out for the 2N3906: its order is reversed (E-B-C), so it would have to be turned around.

R4 can be anything from 1 to 2.2 kΩ. Keep the other values as close as possible to those given. Measure every resistor before soldering it in: it takes 10 seconds and saves hunting for mistakes.

Build order and tests

  1. Lay out the board so the optocouplers sit on the border: boiler side on one side, ESP32 side on the other. Leave a strip of empty holes between them.
  2. Solder the D1–D4 bridge and the screw terminal, then the Zeners. Check where the band is on every diode.
  3. Add Q1 and resistors R1–R3, and the optocouplers (or their sockets) last.
  4. Build the ESP32 side: R4–R7, the LEDs (flat side to the right) and the J2 pin header. Connect the ESP32 with F-F jumpers: J2 pin 1 to GND, 2 to GPIO22, 3 to 3V3, 4 to GPIO21.
  5. Check the isolation with a multimeter in continuity mode. There must be no continuity between any point on the boiler side and the ESP32 GND.
  6. Flash ESPHome with the OpenTherm component to the ESP32 (snippet below). At this stage the adapter is not connected to the boiler yet.
  7. Switch off the boiler power. Disconnect the existing ON/OFF control from the thermostat terminals.
  8. Switch the boiler on with nothing connected to the terminals and measure the DC voltage across them. Write it down. If it reads zero or anything unusual, stop and check before connecting the adapter.
  9. Switch the boiler off again, connect the adapter to the terminals (either polarity), switch the boiler on and watch the ESPHome logs.

According to the manual, the ILEA thermostat inputs are on the safety extra-low-voltage (SELV) connector. Never connect the adapter to any 230 V terminal and do not open the gas section of the boiler. If the boiler does not recognise OpenTherm, check the zone 1 parameters (30–35 in the installer menu). On other boilers, consult the manual for the OpenTherm / room thermostat terminals.
